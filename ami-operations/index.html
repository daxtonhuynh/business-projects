The business outgrew its spreadsheets.

AMI Education, nearly three years after launch.

A system built for a start-up, running a growing business.

Phase 1 was designed for a small team, a handful of classes and one person who knew where every formula lived. It did its job: 70% less administrative work at zero software cost. Then the business grew.

More learners, more classes and more staff meant heavier files, more links between the five workbooks and more people editing at the same time. Links failed to refresh, and a single wrong entry could ripple into class rosters, payroll and both P&L views before anyone noticed.

A larger team also needed what spreadsheets cannot give: personal accounts, approvals and a record of who did what. To keep scaling, and to manage the people behind the numbers, AMI's operating logic needed a new foundation.

Where Excel reached its limits

Volume broke the links

As the workbooks grew, external links between them slowed down and failed to refresh. A warning such as "We couldn't get updated values from a linked workbook" meant receivables, payroll or the P&L were quietly out of date.

Errors cascaded downstream

A mistyped class code or a payment recorded twice flowed automatically into rosters, payroll and both P&L views. Finding the source meant tracing formulas back across five files by hand.

A growing team without accountability

Advisors, teachers and managers shared the same files with no personal accounts and no approvals. Nothing stopped an entry in the wrong area, and there was no field-level record of who changed what.

Month-end relied on discipline

Closing a month meant checking payroll, receipts and expenses by hand, and nothing prevented a closed month from being edited afterwards.

What phase 2 delivers
1database instead of five linked workbooks

No more broken links

Sales, academic, HR and finance read and write the same PostgreSQL database. Reports are live queries rather than copies, so there is nothing to refresh and nothing to fall out of sync.

5role workspaces with personal accounts

Built for a growing team

Each person signs in to a workspace that shows only their work and allows only their actions. Discounts outside policy and receipt voids go to Finance for approval, and every change is logged with who and when.

21automated operational controls

Errors caught at the source

Constraints reject invalid entries before they are saved, and 21 control checks queue what still needs attention: unassigned leads, unconfirmed sessions, overdue instalments, cash differences.

70business-rule tests on every change

Ready to scale

Every rule is covered by automated tests, and a simulated year with 785 leads, 541 enrolments and 1,689 receipts runs end to end through the same rules, including twelve month-end closes.

02 / Solution design

Relational, role-based platform

✓Five workspaces that mirror how AMI worked: Learning Advisors enter sales and receipts; the Academic Manager runs classes; the HR Manager runs payroll; the Finance Manager owns spend, revenue, reporting and the close; the Director sees everything, read-only.
✓Single input, many outputs: a learner, a payment or an hour taught is entered once and flows into rosters, receivables, payroll, revenue and both P&L views, as in phase 1, but through enforced rules instead of copied formulas.

How it works, layer by layer

The technical choices behind the business rules

Data model

93 tables across nine business areas

One schema per area: CRM, academic, billing, HR, payroll, finance, controls, reference data and the app. 175 foreign keys and 116 check constraints hold the records together, and exclusion constraints stop a price or a pay rate from having two overlapping validity periods. The chart of accounts and the P&L layout are data, so Finance can change them without touching code.

Business logic

Rules run inside the database

Business actions such as enrolling a learner, recording a receipt, calculating payroll or closing a month are PL/pgSQL functions (54 in total) backed by 47 triggers. Each validates, writes and derives its results in one transaction. The enrolment screen runs the real function and rolls it back, so the preview shows exactly what will be saved.

Security

Permissions enforced below the app

The application logs in as a user with no rights of its own. Every transaction switches to the signed-in person's database role with SET LOCAL ROLE, so grants and row-level security apply to every query. Write functions run as SECURITY DEFINER with a fixed search path, and passwords are hashed with pgcrypto.

Accounting

Contract-based revenue recognition

Net tuition is spread month by month from the class start (or a late joiner's first month) to the class end; materials are earned up front; drop-outs earn the months served. Deferred revenue is cash kept minus revenue recognised, per enrolment. Cash and accrual P&L share one chart of accounts: write-offs affect accrual only, refunds affect cash only.

Controls & audit

Exceptions, audit trail and period close

21 control checks across sales, billing, academic, payroll and expenses feed a persistent exception queue (open, in progress, resolved, risk accepted). An audit trigger records changes with the user and time. A month closes only after the previous one and only once payroll is approved; closed months reject new receipts, refunds and write-offs.

Quality & delivery

Tested, reproducible, deployable

70 SQL tests cover the business rules, and pytest drives every page for every role plus a full lead-to-close workflow. GitHub Actions runs both on each push. A generator replays a full year day by day through the same functions with a fixed seed, and the platform deploys to Supabase (PostgreSQL) and Streamlit Community Cloud.
